The Problem

AI hiring tools create compliance exposure at every screening step

Resume screening AI uses graduation year, employment gaps, zip codes, and university names as ranking signals — all legal proxies for age, disability, race, and national origin under Title VII, ADEA, and ADA. The EEOC doesn't care if the bias was unintentional.

Where Violations Happen

6 stages of the hiring process. Compliance exposure at every one.

Most HR teams think about EEOC compliance at the offer stage. The liability starts at the first filter.

Stage 1
Resume Screening
HireAI, Workday AI, Greenhouse, Lever
AI ranks 300 applicants, recruiter advances the top 50. Ranking signals include graduation year, university name, employment gaps, and zip code — all legal proxies for age, race, disability, and national origin.
ADEA — graduation year → age proxy Title VII — HBCU excluded from preferred schools list NYC LL144 — bias audit required before use NYC LL144 — 10-day candidate notice required
Stage 2
Skills Assessment
Pymetrics, HireVue Assess, Codility
AI scores cognitive or skills tests. Without reasonable accommodations, timed assessments screen out candidates with disabilities. Cognitive tests trained on younger cohorts produce lower scores for protected age groups.
ADA — no accommodation process for timed assessments ADEA — assessment model trained on younger cohort EEOC UGESP — disparate impact across protected groups
Stage 3
AI Video Interview
HireVue, Spark Hire, Modern Hire
Facial expression analysis and speech pattern scoring run behind what candidates think is a regular video call. Illinois AIVIA requires explicit written consent and the right to request data deletion before any AI video analysis begins.
IL AIVIA — no consent obtained before AI video analysis ADA — speech pattern models penalize speech disabilities ADEA — facial analysis models trained on younger faces
Stage 4
Background Check
Checkr, Sterling, First Advantage
CRA report returns a hit. Recruiter sends a generic rejection email. FCRA §604(b)(3) requires a specific two-step workflow before any rejection is communicated — most companies skip it entirely, creating $1,000/applicant statutory exposure.
FCRA §604(b)(3) — Pre-Adverse Notice not sent before rejection FCRA §615(a) — Final Adverse Notice missing CRA disclosure EEOC — background check criteria must be job-related
Stage 5
Final Hiring Decision
Hiring manager + AI confidence score
Hiring manager rejects based on AI recommendation. The AI's confidence score was shaped by graduation year and an unexplained employment gap — both protected proxies. EU candidates have the right to human review of any automated decision.
ADEA — AI score penalizes graduation year (age proxy) ADA — employment gap penalized without gap reason captured EU AI Act Art. 14 — human oversight not documented EEOC UGESP — selection rate below 80% threshold
Stage 6
Offer & Compensation
Comp bands, salary negotiation, ATS offer
Recruiter anchors comp to the candidate's prior salary — a known proxy for the gender pay gap. In California, New York, Colorado, and Washington, using salary history in compensation decisions is illegal regardless of whether an AI system surfaces it.
CA AB-168 — prior salary used in CA compensation decision NY §194-a — salary history ban violated in NY offer CO EPEWA — pay range must appear in CO job postings
Pragma intercepts at every stage. Pass the decision text and context fields for that stage — the engine runs the applicable regulations automatically based on candidate location, tools used, and decision type.

FCRA Employment Two-Step Adverse Action

When a background check is used in a hiring rejection, Pragma enforces FCRA's two-step workflow: Pre-Adverse Action Notice (§604(b)(3)) with a copy of the report and 5-business-day dispute window, followed by the Final Adverse Action Notice (§615(a)) with CRA identity disclosure. Both notices are generated and ready to send.

FCRA §604(b)(3) · §615(a)
🏙️

NYC LL144 AEDT Enforcement

NYC Local Law 144 requires employers using Automated Employment Decision Tools to conduct annual bias audits, post results publicly, and notify NYC candidates at least 10 business days before screening. Pragma checks all three enforcement workflows and FAILs any missing step.

NYC Admin. Code §20-871

Disparate Impact / UGESP Analysis

Upload a CSV of AI hiring decisions and Pragma computes selection rates per demographic group using the EEOC UGESP 4/5ths rule — the same statistical analysis the EEOC uses in enforcement investigations. Generates audit-ready reports with adverse impact ratios per protected class.

29 CFR Part 1607 · UGESP · EEOC

Using two proxy fields simultaneously (graduation year + zip code, employment gap + university name) creates intersectional discrimination risk higher than any single factor. Pragma detects proxy combinations and flags compound risk with a specific regulatory citation for each pairing.
